This page focuses on drain and sewage backups, because those are the water problems where wrong first steps do the most harm.

Why a backup is different from a pipe leak

Water from a supply line is clean when it leaves the pipe. Water from a toilet, floor drain or sewer line is not. Under the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, sewage is treated as the most contaminated category, and porous materials it touches usually have to be removed.

Stay out of the area, keep children and pets away and do not run water or flush while a backup is active.

Safety steps when sewage backs up

  • Stay out of the affected room and keep children and pets away.
  • Do not run water, flush or use the affected drains.
  • Keep out of any room where water touches outlets or the panel.
  • Wear waterproof boots and gloves if you must enter briefly.
  • Photograph the damage from the doorway before anything is moved.

Who to call first

  • The city Wastewater Department at (972) 780-4900 if the problem may be the public main.
  • A plumber if the blockage is in your own line.
  • Our line at (972) 945-2885 for the cleanup of what the water touched.
  • The electric utility or 911 if water is near outlets or the panel.

How a contaminated cleanup runs

Crews wear protective equipment, set containment to keep the affected area separate and remove saturated carpet, pad and wet drywall that cannot be cleaned. Hard surfaces are cleaned and treated, and drying equipment finishes the job. A household vacuum or ordinary fans are not appropriate for this work.

Read our sewage backup cleanup and flood damage restoration pages, then see water damage cleanup for the removal stage.

Sewer backup coverage and cost

Sewer backup coverage is often an add-on to a homeowners policy, so check your policy and ask your insurer. Photos, dates and a written scope can help describe the loss. Cost depends on how much area was affected, which materials must go and how long drying takes, and the number comes after the space is seen.
