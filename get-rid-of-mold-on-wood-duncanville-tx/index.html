How to Get Rid of Mold on Wood in Duncanville, TX

Surface mold on solid wood can often be cleaned and dried. Wood that is soft, deeply stained or still wet usually has to be replaced.

Written by the Duncanville Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026 · Updated 2026.

To get rid of mold on wood, dry the wood first, then remove the growth with a HEPA vacuum and light sanding or brushing, and only then clean and seal. Wood that stays damp or has gone soft is replaced. Wood is porous, so growth can sit below the surface, and a moisture meter tells you more than your eyes do.

Fix the water problem before touching the mold. Otherwise the growth returns.

Surface growth versus deep growth

Surface mold looks like fuzzy or dusty spots that sit on top of the grain. Deep growth leaves dark staining that reaches into the board, sometimes with a soft, crumbly feel. Press a screwdriver into the wood. If it sinks easily, the wood has lost strength and should be replaced.

Solid lumber that is sound and dry can often be cleaned. Plywood, OSB and particle board swell and delaminate when wet, so they are less forgiving. Furniture with a finish may clean up easily, while raw wood absorbs more.

A moisture meter reading and a probe test decide the line between clean and replace.

Four steps for sound wood

Stop the water

Fix the leak or humidity source and run air movers or a dehumidifier until readings settle.

Remove loose growth

Use a HEPA vacuum first, since a regular shop vac can blow spores around.

Sand or brush

Take off a thin layer with a wire brush or sander and wear a respirator and eye protection.

Clean and seal

Wipe with detergent and water, let it dry fully, then apply a sealer or primer made for wood.

Sanding and sealing are cosmetic if the wood is wet. Dry first, always. Cleaning with a damp rag can drive moisture into the grain, so keep it light and dry the area right away.

Moisture content and why a meter beats your eyes

Wood can look clean and still be wet inside. A moisture meter reads the water content of the board, and the number you want is close to what similar dry wood in the same house reads. A thermal imaging camera also helps by showing cool, wet areas across a ceiling or wall without opening it.

Readings that stay high after the leak is fixed mean water is still arriving, or the drying equipment needs more time. Rough drying logs, kept day by day, show whether the wood is trending toward dry. Ask any company to show you the logs.

Framing, joists and crawl spaces

Framing mold usually follows a roof leak, a plumbing leak or a damp crawl space. If your home has a crawl space or an attic, check there for damp insulation, dark patches and a musty smell. A moisture meter reading on the wood tells you whether it has dried.

Crews use containment barriers, HEPA air scrubbers and sometimes negative air for larger framing jobs, following ANSI/IICRC S520 practice. For wet wood after a leak, water mitigation gets it drying quickly, and mold remediation handles the cleaning when growth is present.

Furniture, decks and fences

Furniture can often be saved if the wood is sound. Move it outside, vacuum with a HEPA vacuum, wipe with detergent, and let it dry in air with the sun on it. Unfinished wood may need light sanding. Upholstered pieces are a separate call, since foam and fabric hold growth.

Decks and fences live outside, so some surface growth is normal. Scrub with detergent and a stiff brush, rinse well and let the boards dry. Seal once dry, and replace boards that are soft or split.

Duncanville Permit & Inspection Services at 203 E. Wheatland Road, (972) 780-5000, says repair work generally needs a permit and inspections. Replacing structural framing is a question to put to the city or the contractor before starting. Checking first keeps the repair from being redone later.

For a simple cleaning of sound wood, you likely do not need to ask. For sistering a joist or swapping studs, ask the department whether your repair needs a permit. A contractor can register in the city's Citizen Access Portal, which is a good question to ask any hire.

What drives the cost of wood repairs

The price depends on how much wood is affected, whether it is framing or finish material, how long the area needs to dry and how much rebuild is needed. The real number comes from an on-site look. Insurance often treats sudden and accidental water as covered and gradual leaks as not, so check your policy and ask your insurer.

Texas mold rules and who does what

Remediation under 25 contiguous square feet is exempt from licensing in Texas. Larger projects use a licensed remediator, and a separate licensed assessor handles the assessment. Ask any company for its TDLR license number and verify it with TDLR. You should receive the TDLR Consumer Mold Information Sheet before work starts.
