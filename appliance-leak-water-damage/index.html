Appliance leaks are usually slow, and the real damage hides under the floor. Shut off the appliance's water valve, unplug it if the area is dry, and call us. We check subfloor moisture and dry what the leak reached. A plumber or appliance technician fixes the machine.

Where appliance leaks come from

Common sources are washer hoses, dishwasher supply and drain lines, and refrigerator ice-maker lines. Hoses age, clamps loosen and drip pans crack. Census counts show 14,227 housing units in Duncanville in 2020, and Texas State Historical Association growth data points to many homes now 45 to 65 years old.

Older homes may have old hoses and tight laundry closets that hide drips.

Safety before you call

  1. Close the shutoff valve behind the appliance.
  2. Unplug it only if the floor and outlet are dry.
  3. Do not stand in water near outlets.
  4. Photograph the leak and the appliance label.
  5. Call us, and a plumber if a supply line failed.

Machine by machine: what each leak tends to do

Washers can release a hose full of pressurized water in minutes if a coupling lets go. Laundry rooms often have hard flooring, so water runs toward the nearest wall. Dishwashers leak slowly from door gaskets, drain hoses and supply fittings, and the water settles under the cabinet base.

Refrigerator ice-maker lines are thin tubes that crack or loosen behind the unit. The drip can run for weeks, and you only notice when the flooring edge cups. Because these leaks are slow, the subfloor is often the wettest part.

How we find and dry hidden moisture

Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera map wet areas under flooring and behind cabinets. Wet vacuums remove standing water. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, and drying logs track daily readings.

We follow ANSI/IICRC S500 as a published reference. For a deeper look at cabinets, see kitchen water damage restoration.

After mapping, the crew decides what can dry in place. Vinyl or laminate over a wet subfloor is often lifted. Cabinet toe kicks may be removed so air reaches the base. Daily readings show when the floor reaches a dry standard and the equipment can leave.

Cost factors for appliance leaks

Cost depends on how long the leak ran and whether flooring or cabinet bases need removal. Subfloor type matters, and so do the number of rooms affected and the days of drying equipment. A leak found early may need only a day or two of drying. No figure is given before the crew sees the space and explains the scope.

Insurance and appliance leaks

Texas Department of Insurance guidance says to notify your insurer promptly. Sudden leaks and long-term seepage are often treated differently, so check your policy. Keep photos, the appliance model label and any repair receipts. Flood damage is a separate matter, and homeowners policies generally do not cover it.

The TDI Consumer Help Line is 1-800-252-3439.

Appliance leak mistakes to avoid

  • Replacing a rubber washer hose that is cracking.
  • Ignoring a damp cabinet base.
  • Covering a wet floor with new flooring.
  • Pushing the washer back without checking for leaks.
  • Using a dehumidifier alone while the subfloor stays wet.

For prevention, replace old washer hoses with braided lines on a schedule, pull the fridge out once a year to check the ice-maker line and look inside the cabinet under the dishwasher. Five minutes of looking beats days of drying.
