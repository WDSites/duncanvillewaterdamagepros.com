Sewage coming up a drain, toilet or shower is treated as Category 3 water, the most contaminated class. Keep people and pets away and call the 24/7 emergency line. We remove the water and affected materials with protective gear and containment, and a plumber or the city handles the blocked line.

What Category 3 means

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard that sorts water by contamination, and sewage falls in Category 3. That means porous items such as carpet, pad and soaked drywall usually cannot be cleaned and are removed. Hard surfaces can be cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products.

Where the backup starts

If only one drain backs up, the problem may be in your own line, and a plumber can clear it. If several fixtures back up, the main line may be blocked, and you should call the city. Public Works Wastewater is at (972) 780-4900, and the city after-hours water line is (972) 780-4959.

Category 3 water can carry bacteria and other contaminants, so the goal is to limit how far it travels. A backup that reached only a bathroom tile floor is a smaller job than one that reached carpet in a hallway. The crew checks where the liquid went, how deep it got, and which materials touched it before choosing what stays and what goes.

Safety first steps

Do not touch the water or wade through it. Turn off power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. Stop using sinks, toilets and the washer, since they add to the flood. Keep children and pets out, and wash hands well if you touched anything.

Right away

  • Stop using water fixtures in the house.
  • Keep people and pets away from the area.
  • Take photos before cleanup begins.
  • Call a plumber or the city about the line.
  • Call the emergency line for cleanup.

Our cleanup process

Crews wear protective gear and set containment barriers to limit spread. Extraction units and wet vacuums remove the liquid. Carpet, pad and soaked drywall come out. Surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, and HEPA air scrubbers and negative air may run during the work.

Where the plumber or the city has cleared the line, we do not run water through the house until it is confirmed open. After cleanup, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure. Large losses can resemble flood damage restoration in scope, and finishing steps overlap with water damage cleanup.

The crew cuts drywall back above the wet line when contaminated water wicked upward. Baseboards, pad and tack strip are bagged and carried out, and the sealed bags leave through a path that does not cross clean rooms. Floors and framing that remain are cleaned, treated and then dried to the drying goal with readings logged each visit.

Cost factors for sewage cleanup

Cost depends on the area affected, the depth of the water, how much porous material must be removed, the protective gear and containment needed, and the days of drying. The figure comes after the crew sees the space.

Insurance for sewage backup

Texas Department of Insurance guidance says to notify your agent or company as soon as you know of damage. Many policies treat a sewer or drain backup differently from other water losses, so read yours closely or ask your agent what applies. The TDI Consumer Help Line is 1-800-252-3439.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Cleaning it up with a household mop and bare hands.
  • Keeping wet carpet because it looks fine.
  • Running fans that spread contaminated air.
  • Assuming a plumber alone will handle cleanup.
