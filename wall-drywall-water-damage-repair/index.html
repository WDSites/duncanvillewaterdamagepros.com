Wet drywall usually has to be cut out when it is soft, stained or bulging, or when readings stay high. The cut goes above the wet line, the cavity is dried, and only then is the wall closed and repaired.

This page is for non-emergency work. If water is running, shut off the supply and use the 24/7 emergency line.

Why walls hold water in older homes

Texas State Historical Association data show Duncanville grew fast between 1960 and 1980, so many homes have older walls, original plumbing and aged seals. Water in a wall can come from a pipe, a window or a roof.

The National Weather Service Fort Worth office notes that Gulf moisture keeps summer humidity high, so a damp cavity dries slowly without equipment.

The practical point is that a wall can look fine while the cavity is wet. Board hides insulation, studs and bottom plates, and those stay damp long after the paint dries. A meter reading is the only honest way to know.

Safety around wet walls

Wet walls can hide wiring. Do not cut into a wall near outlets or switches. Turn off power to the area at the panel if it is dry and safe to reach.

If a pipe is leaking in the wall, close the main shutoff and call a plumber. We dry what the leak leaves.

Flood cuts, insulation and baseboards

A flood cut is a straight cut across the wall at a set height above the wet line. We usually go a few inches above the highest moisture reading, so the board that remains is dry.

Wet insulation is removed because it holds water against the framing. Baseboards come off first and often can be reused if they are not swollen.

Measure

Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera find the wet line.

Cut

Board is cut above that line, and wet insulation comes out.

Dry

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the studs and cavity.

Verify

Readings in the drying log confirm the framing is dry.

Drying the cavity, then the repair

Closing a wall too soon traps moisture. After readings are normal, new board is hung, taped and mudded. The patch is textured to match the wall and painted.

We hand you a clear rebuild scope listing each step, so you know what is included. For broader repair work, see water damage repair. For the equipment side, read structural drying.

Ask the City of Duncanville Permit and Inspection Services whether your repair needs a permit. The department says repair work generally needs a permit and inspections.

Repair has its own steps. Patch seams are taped and coated in thin layers that need time to dry between coats. Texture is matched to the room, and primer goes on before paint so the patch does not show through.

When to call a plumber or roofer first

Water in a wall always has a source. If a supply line is leaking, the plumber stops it. If rain is coming through a roof or window, a roofer or window installer closes the opening.

We tell you honestly when the source still needs a trade, because drying a wall that is still getting wet only wastes time. Once the source is fixed, the cavity can be dried properly.

Cost factors for wall repair

The price depends on the wall area, the number of rooms, insulation, trim, the days of drying and the texture and paint scope. Exact figures come after the crew sees the walls and measures the wet area. The rebuild scope is written out before work begins.

Insurance for wall damage

Texas Department of Insurance guidance says to notify your agent or company as soon as you are aware of damage. Sudden pipe failure is often handled differently from slow seepage.

Check your policy and photograph the wall before it is opened. Our crews work with ANSI/IICRC S500 as the published reference.

Mistakes to avoid with wet walls

  • Painting over a stain before the source is fixed.
  • Closing the wall without readings.
  • Leaving wet insulation in the cavity.
  • Cutting only to the visible stain.
  • Sealing the wall before a plumber checks the pipe.
