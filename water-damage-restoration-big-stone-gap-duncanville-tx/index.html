Why association rules matter after a leak

Homes in an owners association may have rules about exterior work, shared walls, shared plumbing or who handles what. Before work starts, read your association documents or ask the management company who is responsible for the damaged area.

Tell the association promptly if the water came from a neighbor's unit or a shared line, and keep a record of what you reported and when.

Your first hour in an HOA home

Safety steps for the first hour

  • Close the shutoff for the leaking fixture or the main valve if it is dry and reachable.
  • Keep people and pets out of standing water.
  • Do not touch breakers or plug in equipment while standing in water.
  • Photograph the damage and the source before moving belongings.
  • Call for help and tell dispatch whether the water came from your unit or elsewhere.

How the drying work runs

Standing water is extracted first. Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera then show where water traveled inside walls and floors. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, and drying logs record the readings day by day.

For the details, see water mitigation, structural drying and emergency water removal. Wet drywall is covered on the drywall repair page.

Documentation for your insurer and association

Photos with dates, moisture readings and a written scope of work can help describe a loss to an insurer and to an association. They do not guarantee that a claim is paid, and coverage depends on the cause and your policy. Texas Department of Insurance guidance says to notify your company as soon as you know about damage. Ask any restoration company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published reference for water damage restoration.
