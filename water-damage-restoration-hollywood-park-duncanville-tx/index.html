What water damage usually looks like inside a home

Most calls from a residential street like these start inside the house, not outside it. A supply line under a sink gives way, a washing machine hose slips, a water heater tank weeps at the base, or a dishwasher leaks into the subfloor. None of these need a storm, and none depend on where the house sits.

The water moves down and out. It runs under cabinets and baseboards, wicks up drywall and soaks the pad under carpet. A floor that looks dry on top can stay wet underneath for days.

Safety before anything else

Close the main water shutoff if you can reach it without stepping into water. Keep people and pets out of standing water, because it can hide electrical hazards. Do not use breakers or plug in equipment while standing in water, and call the electric utility or 911 for any immediate danger.

For a water main or service line problem outside the house, the City of Duncanville takes after-hours water emergencies at (972) 780-4959. Photograph the damage before moving belongings if it is safe to do so.

How extraction and drying work

Crews remove standing water with wet vacuums, extraction units or submersible pumps, depending on depth. Then they read moisture in walls, floors and framing with a moisture meter and look for hidden wet areas with a thermal imaging camera. Air movers and dehumidifiers come next and run until the readings drop, which is tracked in a drying log.

Our emergency water removal and water mitigation pages explain both stages in more detail. Many jobs also need cleanup of soaked materials and, later, repair.

Floodplain facts and what we cannot say

The City of Duncanville floodplain page names Ten Mile Creek, Horne Branch and Mauk Branch and says about 480 acres of the city are floodplain. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified for Hollywood Park, so we make no claim about whether your street is mapped.

The city's Floodplain Administrator can check a specific property at (972) 707-3856. Homeowners policies generally do not cover flood damage, according to the Texas Department of Insurance, so ask your insurer what your policy says.

Insurance documentation

Photos of the damage, the date you first saw it and a list of affected items help describe a loss. Moisture readings and a written scope can support a restoration record. Whether the loss is covered depends on the cause and your policy terms, so contact your insurer promptly.

What changes the cost

Cost depends on the water category, how many rooms and materials are wet, how many days of drying equipment are needed and what must be removed. The number comes after someone has seen the space, so we do not quote prices on this page.
