Cleanup is what happens once the water has stopped. It removes ruined carpet, pad, insulation and debris, cleans what can be saved and sanitizes surfaces that touched dirty water. The first 48 hours matter most, so if your house is wet right now, call the 24/7 emergency line.

Safety first. Stop the source, keep people and pets out of water that may be contaminated and stay away from wet electrical equipment. Photograph everything before it is moved.

What cleanup removes and what it saves

The category of water decides most of the plan. Clean water from a broken supply line lets crews save more materials. Water from a drain, a washing machine or outside carries contaminants, and porous materials such as carpet pad, insulation and drywall are usually removed because they cannot be cleaned reliably.

Hard, sealed surfaces such as tile, sealed concrete and many cabinets can be cleaned and kept. Crews bag and haul away what has to go so the room can dry without wet material holding moisture.

Category rules follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

Sanitizing, deodorizing and the smell of old water

After removal, surfaces are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products suited to the material. Deodorizing targets the musty smell that sets in once materials stay damp. Air scrubbers with HEPA filters can clean the air in rooms that held contaminated water.

A lingering smell after cleanup usually means moisture is still inside a wall or under flooring. That is a sign to dry further with water mitigation before any rebuild.

The first 48 hours: an emergency checklist

What to do while you wait

  • Shut off the main water valve if the source is still running.
  • Cut power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  • Lift valuables, electronics and rugs out of the water.
  • Open windows if the weather allows and run fans for air movement.
  • Keep children and pets out of sewage or flood water.
  • Take photos of every wet area before anything is thrown away.

Documentation for your claim

Photos, a list of damaged items and written moisture readings help your insurer see what happened. We document the loss as it is cleaned. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by homeowners policies, gradual leaks often are not, and flood usually needs separate coverage.

Ask your insurer what it requires and how soon to report, since some policies set short notice windows for water damage. We never promise that a claim will be approved.

What changes the cleanup price

Five things move the price: the water category, the number of square feet affected, the materials removed, the drying days and equipment, and how much rebuild follows. Haul-away volume matters too. The scope and price are explained before work starts.
