Flood water is different from a leaking pipe. It carries soil, bacteria and sometimes sewage, so treat it as contaminated until it is proven otherwise. If water is entering your home now, move to a dry area, stay out of the water and call the 24/7 emergency line.

If water has reached outlets, the electrical panel or gas appliances, do not walk through it. Call the utility or 911 first, then call us.

When flood water is an emergency

Flood water is an emergency from the first inch. It soaks flooring, drywall and insulation fast, and the longer it sits the more has to be removed. Crews work in protective gear, pump or extract the water, remove porous materials that cannot be sanitized and start drying.

After hours and weekends are treated like any other time, and same-day response is offered when available. We never promise an arrival time, because roads and conditions in a storm change it.

Basement flooding cleanup

If your home has a basement, a walkout lower level or a crawl space, that is where groundwater and heavy rain usually show up first. Water enters through window wells, floor cracks, a failed sump pump or a backing-up drain.

The crew extracts the water, removes wet carpet and the bottom of wet drywall, and sets drying equipment. Cutting the wall above the waterline lets air reach the framing. Basement waterproofing is a separate trade, and restoration is not waterproofing, so a contractor for that work may be the next call.

Sewage backup cleanup: what to do first

A sewage backup is Category 3 water. Stay out, keep children and pets away, and do not run water or flush. Call the line. If the sewer main outside the house is the problem, the City of Duncanville Public Works Wastewater Department can be reached at (972) 780-4900.

Crews wear protective equipment, remove all affected porous material, clean and disinfect hard surfaces, and run air filtration. Anything soft that touched sewage generally goes, because it cannot be cleaned to a safe standard.

Local flood exposure in Duncanville

The city of Duncanville has a Floodplain Management page and a drainage master plan survey, and it names no flood zones there. We do not guess your risk. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center by street address, and ask the city whether your property sits in a mapped floodplain.

Duncanville sits on Tenmile Creek and its tributaries, including Stewart Branch, near Harrington Park, where a sewer overflow occurred in 2023. That event is a useful reminder that creek-side areas and the sewer system are both part of local water risk. For neighbors north of us, Grand Prairie publishes its own floodplain plan.

Insurance and flood coverage

Homeowners policies generally do not cover flood damage, according to the Texas Department of Insurance. Flood coverage is usually a separate policy. The 2021 winter storm showed that federal help can also be available after a declared disaster, and FEMA designated Dallas County for Individual Assistance that year.

Read your policy and ask your insurer what applies. Document the loss with photos, and we add moisture readings and a written scope.

What drives the price of flood restoration

Water category, depth and square footage, the materials soaked, drying days and equipment, and the rebuild all shape the cost. The scope and price are explained before work starts.
